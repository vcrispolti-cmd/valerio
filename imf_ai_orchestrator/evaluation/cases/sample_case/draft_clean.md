# Country A: Energy-sector arrears fell sharply, but the drivers cannot yet be separated

<!-- SYNTHETIC TEST CASE: fictional country and data, for testing the orchestrator only. -->

## Main findings

Energy-sector arrears fell from 4.1 percent of GDP at end-2023 to 2.6 percent of GDP at end-2025. [C001] Collection rates rose from 78 percent in 2023 to 86 percent in 2025. [C002] The cash waterfall mechanism was introduced in March 2024. [C003] In 2025, the Treasury cleared arrears equivalent to 0.9 percent of GDP through a securitization operation. [C004]

## Interpretation

The cash waterfall appears to have contributed to the reduction in arrears, alongside higher collections and the 2025 arrears clearance. [C005] Because these factors moved together, their separate contributions cannot be identified with the available data.

## Tariffs and risks

Average end-user tariffs remained about 20 percent below the cost-recovery level in 2025. [C006] Arrears could re-emerge if collection gains are not sustained. [C008]

## Staff Appraisal

Staff recommends gradually moving tariffs toward cost-recovery levels, with targeted support for vulnerable households. [C007]
