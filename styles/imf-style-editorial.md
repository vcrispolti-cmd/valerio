# IMF-Style: Editorial Rules (mechanics)

This is the companion to `styles/imf-style.md`. It condenses the **IMF Editorial Style Guide, 2nd edition (2024)**, issued by the Publishing Division of the IMF Communications Department (COMPD).

For anything not covered here, follow the *Chicago Manual of Style*, 17th edition, and *Merriam-Webster's Collegiate Dictionary*, 11th edition.

## Scope: which rules apply to which product

The Editorial Style Guide governs **publications**: formal ones (flagships, books, SDNs, Departmental Papers, Staff Climate Notes) and informal ones (Working Papers, IMF Notes). Internal and Board documents follow **staff-report conventions** that differ in a few places. The samples show these clearly.

| Rule | Publications (default) | Staff reports, policy notes, Board papers |
|---|---|---|
| Name of the institution | "the IMF" (never "the Fund"; "in IMF documents") | "the Fund" / "the IMF" both acceptable |
| Bold lead sentences | Remove them ("unbold boldfaced introductory sentences") | Use them; each numbered paragraph opens with a bold judgment |
| Headings | Unnumbered, headline-style | Roman-numeral parts, lettered sections, numbered paragraphs |
| US dollars | "$100" (US dollar is assumed); "US$" only if the distinction matters | "US$100" is common |
| Quarters in text | "in the fourth quarter of 2022", "the second half of 2026" | "Q4", "2027H1", "Q4/Q4" tolerated |
| Staff | "the IMF staff", with a plural verb | "staff", no article ("staff's advice", "staff recommends") |

**Rule of thumb.** For anything intended for publication or external readers, apply this file fully. For a staff report or policy note, apply this file except for the right-hand column above.

---

## 1. Grammar and punctuation

- **Serial comma**, always: "electricity, sanitation, and telecommunication."
- **No contractions** (except in proper nouns).
- **Semicolons** go between list items that themselves contain commas.
- **Horizontal (run-in) lists** use (1), (2), (3): "topics including (1) the expenditure needs…, (2) digitalization, and (3) mitigating…" Use a colon before a list only if the text before it is a complete sentence. Eliminate unnecessary numbering in short run-in lists.
- **Vertical lists** take a complete introductory sentence ending in a colon, then bullets. Capitalize the first word of each bullet even if it is not a full sentence. Use numbers only for sequences.
  - A paragraph or sentence with **more than five** numbered or lettered items should become a bulleted list.
- **Quotation marks** are double, with single quotes inside. Periods and commas go inside the closing quotation mark. Use double quotes to define or flag a term at first use ("servicification").
- **Parentheses** set off material; use brackets for a parenthetical inside a parenthetical.
- **Slashes:**
  - No spaces around a slash.
  - Fiscal years: "FY24/25", "fiscal year 2022/23", "2002/3".
  - **Avoid "and/or" and "him/her"**: choose one, or use the plural "their".
- **American spelling**, except in proper nouns: "modeling", "catalog", "judgment", "gray", "check", "license", "canceled", "traveled", "totaled", "benefited", "enrollment", "sizable", "toolkit".
- Keep diacritics in names of people and organizations: "Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development", "Médecins Sans Frontières".

## 2. Hyphens and dashes

- **Hyphenate a phrasal adjective before a noun** but not after it: "a well-trained economist" / "the economist was well trained"; "weaker-than-expected results" / "results were weaker than expected"; "medium-term outlook" / "over the medium term".
- **Ratios** take hyphens throughout, not en dashes: "debt-to-income ratio", "gross-public-debt-to-GDP ratio".
- **En dash (–)** is used for:
  - ranges: "1978–79", "2012–13", "1990–2010"
  - votes: "6–3"
  - items of equal weight: "public–private partnership", "interest–growth differential"
  - compound modifiers with an open compound: "World Bank–financed loans", "anti–money laundering", "post–COVID-19"
  - a hyphenated term used as an adjective: "COVID-19–related goods"
- **Em dash (—)**, with no spaces, marks an abrupt break or interruption: "…poverty and—together with the effects of climate change—impeded growth…"
- **Fractions and time units:** "one-half", "three-quarters", "a three-and-a-half-year waiting period".

## 3. Capitalization and headings

- **Headings and titles** use headline-style capitalization at every level. Capitalize nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs. Lowercase all prepositions and conjunctions regardless of length.
  - Example: "Sub-Saharan Africa: Growth Prospects Are Strong, but Risks Remain"
- **Do not number headings** in publications.
- **Capitalize cross-references to numbered elements:** Chapter, Annex, Appendix, Box, Figure, Table, Map, Part ("As Chapter 3 shows…", "Box 10.2", "Table 3.4").
  - Do **not** capitalize "panel" or "equation": "panel 3 in Figure 4.2", "equation 3.7", "(Figure 1.6, panel 2)".
- **Specific titles, units, and official forms** are capitalized: "Senior Research Officer John Smith", "US Census 2010". Generic uses are lowercase: "a census form".
- **Do not refer to "above" and "below"** for document sections. Write "As discussed in the previous section" or "As discussed in the 'Methods' section".

## 4. Numbers, percentages, and units

- **Spell out numbers below 10**; use numerals for 10 and above: "two countries". When numbers are mixed, use numerals for all: "in 2 out of 20 countries".
- **Always use numerals** for percentages, ratios, statistical expressions, currency with symbols, decades, and large numbers from millions up: "3 percent", "a ratio of 3 to 1", "a 3:1 ratio", "8 deaths per 1,000", "$1 a day", "the 1990s", "3 billion people".
- **"percent", never "%"**, except where space does not permit (figures and tables).
- **Percentage point:** singular for values of 1 or less, plural above 1: "0.25 percentage point", "1.25 percentage points".
- **Fractions** are acceptable even alongside decimals: "6¼ percentage points", "1½ percent".
- **Basis points:** "a 100 basis point increase" (no hyphen); "an increase of 100 basis points".
- **Commas** go in numbers above 999: "1,000".
- **Units of measure** are spelled out in text, boxes, and annexes, and abbreviated only where space is tight (figures).
- **Mathematical operators:**
  - Put spaces around =, −, ×, ÷: "maximum = 7".
  - No space after relational signs: "<5 countries", "±3 percent margin of error".
- **Statistics notation:**
  - Write "p value" (italic *p*, no hyphen), "t-test", "F-statistic", "z-score", "R²", "chi-square test".
  - Use italic *t* for time: "year *t* − 1".
  - Greek letters are roman; matrices and vectors are bold.

## 5. Currencies

- **The US dollar is assumed:** write "$100", not "US$100" or "USD100" (see the staff-report exception above). If the distinction is necessary, use "US$", never "USD".
- Mark other dollars as in the IMF Official Country and Currency Terms: "Can$100", "$NZ".
- **Repeat the symbol in ranges:** "$8 billion to $130 billion", "$8,000–$130,000".
- **Do not convert** multiple currencies into one; mixing them is allowed.
- **Renminbi** is the currency and **yuan** is the unit: "The renminbi depreciated"; "a few hundred yuan".
- Write "special drawing right" in lowercase.

## 6. Dates and time

- **Date format:** month, day, year, as in "January 12, 2024". Write years with four digits.
- **Year ranges in the same century:** "2012–13". Across centuries: "1990–2010".
- **Quarters, halves, and months:**
  - In text, use words: "in the fourth quarter of 2022", "the second half of 2026".
  - In figures and tables only, use colon forms: "1975:Q4", "2010:H2", "2014:M12".
- **"end-"** forms such as "end-December" appear only in tables and figures. In text, write "the end of June" or "June 30, 2014".
- **Time of day:** "AM" and "PM", capitalized and without periods.
- **Avoid vague time references:** "in 2012" is better than "a decade ago". Use "past" for a former occurrence and "last" for a final one.

## 7. Latin and foreign terms

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| e.g. | for example |
| i.e. | that is |
| etc. | and so on |
| et al. | and others |
| c.f. | compare |
| ceteris paribus | all else equal |

Keep "de jure" and "de facto" for exchange rate arrangements.

## 8. Abbreviations

- **Three-use rule.** Spell out at the first mention and use the abbreviation thereafter, **only if** it appears three or more times in that section or subpart (chapter, box, annex, figure, table, note). If it is used only once or twice, spell it out each time.
- **Define abbreviations again in each chapter** and in each box, figure, or table note.
- **Eliminate ad hoc abbreviations** however often they appear, for example "GFC" for "global financial crisis". Never reuse one abbreviation for two terms.
- **Articles:** use "the" before abbreviations pronounced as letters ("the IMF", "the OECD") and no article before those pronounced as words ("UNESCO", "NATO").
- **No periods:** "US", "UK", "EU", "UN", "OECD". "US" and "UK" are **adjectives only**: write "the United States" as a noun and "US banks" as an adjective.
- **Always spell out:**
  - global financial crisis
  - sub-Saharan Africa
  - Fiscal Affairs Department
  - Finance Department
  - *World Economic Outlook*
  - *Global Financial Stability Report*
  - *Fiscal Monitor*
  - Multilateral Consultation
- **Always abbreviate:** GDP, COVID-19, COVAX, Eurostat, ChatGPT.
- Common abbreviations (spelled out at first use): AE (advanced economy), EMDE, LIC, FCS, AML/CFT (anti–money laundering/combating the financing of terrorism), SDR, DSA, NBFI, CBDC, AI (artificial intelligence).

| inflation pressure | inflationary pressure |
| interestingly | it is interesting that (or cut) |
| long run / medium run / short run | long term / medium term / short term |
| utilize | use |
| via | through |

| resiliency | resilience |
| sectorial | sectoral |
| corporates | firms; corporations |
| frictions | friction |
| the Fed | Federal Reserve |

| eurozone / euro zone | euro area |

**Use "while" only for time.** Write "whereas" or "although" if "while" could be ambiguous.

**Use "compared with" for differences** and "compared to" for primarily similarities.

## 11. Boxes, figures, and tables

**Every box, figure, and table** needs a number, a title, a source line, and an in-text callout ("as shown in Box 10.2", "(Figure 1.6, panel 2)"). Every figure panel needs a number and a title.

**Titles:**
- Use headline-style capitalization and a "what–where–when" structure.
- Analytical figures may add a **narrative head**, a sentence stating the takeaway (GFSR Figure 1.1: "A Banking Turmoil Jolted Markets").
- Put the unit line under the title, in parentheses, in sentence case, and italicized, for example *(Percent of GDP)*.
- A continued element repeats only its number plus *(continued)*.

**Numbering:**
- In a chapter, number by chapter: Box 1.1, Figure 2.1, Table 3.2.
- Inside a box: Figure 2.1.1. In an annex: Annex Figure 3.1.1. In an appendix: Appendix Figure 2.1.
- Without chapters, number consecutively.
- Panels are numbered 1, 2, 3, never "a/b".

**Source and note lines, in this order:**
1. "Source:" / "Sources:" (unbolded). List sources alphabetically, separated by semicolons, with "and" before the last, and **"IMF staff calculations" last**. Example: *Sources: Haver Analytics; IMF, World Economic Outlook database; and IMF staff calculations.*
2. "Note:" (always singular, unbolded). Define abbreviations alphabetically with "=": *Note: CIT = corporate income tax; PIT = personal income tax; VAT = value-added tax.*
3. Numbered footnotes for elements marked with superscripts.
4. Significance asterisks last: *\*p < .10; \*\*p < .05; \*\*\*p < .01.* (list only the levels used).

**Author-made graphics:** the source line reads "Source: Authors." or "Authors, based on Reinhart and Rogoff 2009."

**Figures:**
- Legends, line labels, and axis titles use sentence case, with units in lowercase parentheses.
- Every component's unit of measure must be clear.
- With many country labels, use ISO three-letter codes and add the note "Data labels in the figure use International Organization for Standardization (ISO) country codes."

**Maps:**
- Avoid borders under dispute.
- Any world map must carry the standard footnote: "The boundaries, colors, denominations, and any other information shown on the maps do not imply, on the part of the International Monetary Fund, any judgment on the legal status of any territory or any endorsement or acceptance of such boundaries."

**Tables:** no blank cells (except in regression tables), every column headed, and headline-style headings.

**Boxes:** at most about 800 words. Footnotes restart at 1 in each box. Flagship boxes credit authors in an unnumbered footnote: "The authors of this box are…"

**Do not duplicate:** cut text that repeats a figure or table, and cut figures or tables that repeat the text. Text, tables, and figures must agree.

## 12. Citations and references (Chicago author–date)

**In-text citations:**
- One to three authors, list all: "(Bandara and Yu 2003)", "Dollar, Kleineberg, and Kraay (2016)". Four or more: "Mahler and others (2021)". Never write "et al."
- Order multiple citations **oldest to newest**, separated by semicolons: "(World Bank 2004; UNCTAD 2018; IMF 2023)".
- Use page numbers for direct quotes: "(Schindler 2014, p. 123)".
- Forthcoming work: "(IMF, forthcoming-a)". Multiple works in a year: "IMF 2023a, 2023b".
- **Do not hyperlink text.** Put URLs in parentheses.
- A **citation-only footnote should move into the text** as an author–date callout. Use footnotes for explanatory text or for sources with no author–date form (databases, surveys, country authorities).

**IMF flagships:**
- Cited **inside flagships or REOs**, they go by month, year, and title, and are not added to the references: "The April 2023 *Fiscal Monitor* indicated that…"
- Cited in **other publications**, they are treated like any source: "IMF (2023)", with a reference entry.

**Reference list:**
- Each chapter gets its own list. Do not use three em dashes for repeat authors. List seven authors, then "and others", for works with eight or more. Do not mix full first names and initials.
- Write "Unpublished" (not "Mimeo"). Include https:// in URLs and DOIs where feasible, with no final period and no "Available at". Newspapers go in footnotes only.
- Avoid citing internal IMF documents.

**Example forms:**
- *Journal:* Abeysinghe, Tilak, and Kristin Forbes. 2005. "Trade Linkages and Output-Multiplier Effects: A Structural VAR Approach with a Focus on Asia." *Review of International Economics* 13 (2): 356–75.
- *IMF Working Paper:* Araujo, Juliana D., Antonio C. David, Carlos van Hombeeck, and Chris Papageorgiou. 2015. "Non-FDI Capital Inflows in Low-Income Developing Countries: Catching the Wave?" IMF Working Paper 15/86, International Monetary Fund, Washington, DC.
- *SDN:* Aiyar, Shekhar, Jiaqian Chen, Christian Ebeke, Roberto Garcia-Saltos, Tryggvi Gudmundsson, Anna Ilyina, Alvar Kangur, and others. 2023. "Geoeconomic Fragmentation and the Future of Multilateralism." IMF Staff Discussion Note 23/001, International Monetary Fund, Washington, DC.
- *Country Report:* International Monetary Fund (IMF). 2026. "United States: 2026 Article IV Consultation—Press Release; Staff Report; and Statement by the Executive Director for the United States." IMF Country Report 26/76, Washington, DC.

**Italicize publication titles:** *World Economic Outlook*, *Global Financial Stability Report*, *Fiscal Monitor*, *External Sector Report*. Database names stay roman: "World Economic Outlook database", "International Financial Statistics database".

## 13. Annexes and appendixes

- An **Appendix** belongs to the whole document: Appendix 1, Appendix 2.
- An **Annex** belongs to one chapter: Annex 5.1, Annex 5.2.

## 14. Copyediting pass (from the Guide's checklists)

- [ ] Factual inconsistencies, faulty logic, incomplete comparisons, and vague time references are fixed.
- [ ] Redundant passages and long sentences are cut. Long strings of modifiers before nouns are simplified.
- [ ] Passive constructions are replaced with active verbs where appropriate. Excess words that do not advance the argument are removed.
- [ ] Jargon is reduced or defined. Unclear "this/that/these" antecedents and "we/you" usage are clarified.
- [ ] Transitions between paragraphs and sections are smooth. Tone is consistent across authors.
- [ ] Headings are informative and parallel.
- [ ] Abbreviations are minimized and defined per chapter; ad hoc ones are removed.
- [ ] Every figure, table, and box is called out by number, sourced, has units, and agrees with the text.
- [ ] Citations match the references.
- [ ] Country names follow IMF conventions and are listed alphabetically unless there is a reason not to.
- [ ] Spelling, hyphenation, capitalization, and number style follow §§1–10.
- [ ] Contractions, "e.g./i.e./etc./et al.", "and/or", "via", "utilize", "impact" (as a general word), and "due to" (not after a verb) are removed.
